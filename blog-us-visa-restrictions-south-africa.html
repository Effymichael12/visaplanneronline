New US Visa Restriction Policies on South Africa: Who's Actually Affected

On 15 September 2026, the United States introduced new visa restrictions tied to South Africa. The announcement has understandably caused concern — here's who the policy actually targets, why it was introduced, and why South African students and visitors have nothing to worry about.

Visa Planner Online
Published 5 October 2026 · 4 min read Expert Reviewed

On 15 September 2026, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio introduced new visa restrictions affecting certain South African individuals. The announcement has caused a wave of understandable concern among South Africans with pending or future U.S. visa applications. Here's a clear breakdown of who the policy actually applies to, why it was introduced, and — most importantly — who isn't affected by it.

Who the Restrictions Apply To

According to the Secretary of State's statement, the travel ban applies to certain South African individuals linked to race-based policies against the country's white community. It also extends to any individual who has supported or taken part in land expropriation from white South African landowners.

Why the Policy Was Introduced

The U.S. government has previously communicated its disapproval directly to the South African president regarding what it characterizes as government-sponsored discrimination against the country's white community. According to the U.S. government's stated position, policies coming out of South Africa have contributed to serious and violent crime against farming communities in rural areas.

The ban explicitly targets individuals who are enabling or advancing the Expropriation Act, which permits uncompensated land seizures from white farming communities. The policy has also been framed, by the U.S. government, as a response to South Africa's Black Economic Empowerment rules, which it considers unfair to the country's minority population.

Who Is Not Affected

Students and regular visitors are not affected by this policy. It does not restrict South African student visas or visitor visas in any way. The ban is specifically aimed at politicians, lawmakers and officials whom the United States has determined to be responsible for enacting laws that contribute to violence against the country's minority race.

Key Takeaway

If you're a South African student planning to study in the United States, or simply travelling as a visitor, this policy does not change your eligibility or application process. The restrictions are narrowly targeted at specific public officials, not at the general South African population. As with any policy shift, it's worth staying informed as the situation develops.
