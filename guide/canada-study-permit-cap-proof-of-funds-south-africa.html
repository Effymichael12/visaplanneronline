Canada Cuts Study Permits by 49% & Raises Living Expense Proof | 2026/2027 Alert

If you are a South African planning to pursue your tertiary education in Canada, significant regulatory shifts are reshaping the study permit landscape.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) has announced major policy updates that impact study permit caps, financial proof thresholds, and off-campus work compliance rules. If you are preparing a Canada visa South Africa application, staying ahead of these updates is crucial to avoiding processing delays or application refusals.

1. Massive 49% reduction in Canadian study permits

Starting for the Fall 2026 intake and continuing into 2027, Canada is capping its national target for international study permits at 309,670 per year.

This represents a 49% reduction compared to previous peak levels. With significantly fewer total visas being allocated globally, admission and permit approvals are becoming noticeably more competitive. South African applicants need carefully structured applications—including flawless Letters of Explanation (LoE) and verified Provincial Attestation Letters (PAL)—to secure their permit in this tighter environment.

2. Updated financial living cost requirements

Alongside the permit cap, IRCC has officially adjusted its proof-of-funds requirement to keep pace with Canadian living costs.

  1. New living allowance baseline: South African primary applicants must now show at least $23,448 CAD (approximately R271,621) in unencumbered, liquid funds to cover first-year living expenses.
  2. Tuition & travel extra: This amount is strictly for living expenses and must be demonstrated in addition to your first-year tuition fees and travel allowance.

For detailed guidelines on accepted bank documentation and sponsor requirements, see our comprehensive breakdown on Canada visa requirements South Africa.

3. Tighter off-campus work policies

While Canada continues to offer international students off-campus work eligibility, compliance standards are tightening:

  1. 24-hour weekly limit: Students are permitted to work off-campus for up to 24 hours per week during active academic terms (and full-time during official summer/winter breaks).
  2. Compliance checks: IRCC and institutions are monitoring full-time enrollment status more closely. Dropping below full-time status or exceeding the weekly hour cap can jeopardize your legal status and future post-graduation work opportunities.
