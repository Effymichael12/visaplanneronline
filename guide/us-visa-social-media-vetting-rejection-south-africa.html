Key US visa social media rules & rejection categories

Applicants submitting DS-160 forms at the Johannesburg (Sandton) or Cape Town consulates must ensure their public social media presence aligns with their stated non-immigrant intent. Automated AI scanners review public posts across major platforms for inconsistencies.

Private profile flag

Setting listed social handles to private during application review causes automatic evasion flags. Maintain public profile status and ensure truthful, clean posts.

Mass deletion flag

Wiping historical posts or comments before interview submission triggers warnings. Do not mass delete; AI logs record historical data activity.

INA Section 214(b)

Failure to prove strong economic or social ties to South Africa leads to rejection. Provide employment letters, property deeds and family proof.

INA Section 221(g)

Incomplete documentation or missing biometrics/police records freezes your file. Complete a thorough document audit before attending the consulate.

Avoiding INA 214(b) and social media evasion flags

Consular officers evaluate non-immigrant visa applications under the assumption that every applicant intends to stay permanently in the United States unless proven otherwise. Statements on social media about job searching, long-term stays or unofficial travel changes directly contradict DS-160 declarations.
