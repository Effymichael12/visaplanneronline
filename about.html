Our Story

I Learned This the Hard Way, So You Don't Have To.

Eight years navigating US immigration taught me that getting the offer is the easy part. Here's the story behind why Visa Planner Online exists, and why it's built the way it is.

Founder, Visa Planner Online
US, UK & Canada Visa Specialist 8 Years of Lived Experience

I spent the last eight years of my life in the United States. I went in on a student visa in 2018 for my bachelor's degree, and after graduating I completed my master's and worked through OPT for a year. The process was so straightforward for me when applying for the visa, because of how well I prepared. My application was approved at the US embassy in India after just three questions. The move itself was smooth.

Then One Wrong Answer Changed Everything

One time I visited India and, on my return flight to the United States, I was stopped at immigration because I gave the wrong answer — nothing dramatic, just anxiety and exhaustion from the trip catching up with me at the worst possible moment. I was sent to secondary security check, where officials went through my bank statements, my phone, and every transaction, thoroughly.

After that, I had a real fear of the system, and constant paranoia whenever I travelled — even domestically, within the country I was already living in legally.

The Turning Point

That fear is also what pushed me to actually learn immigration law and policy, properly, instead of just hoping for the best next time. It paid off: I was later granted the opportunity to work off-campus during my master's programme — something genuinely rare — because of how well I understood the rules and how to work within them.

Why This Matters for South Africans

South Africans aren't always aware of how serious the US visa process actually is. Getting an offer — to visit family, attend a conference, or study — is the easy part, and it isn't even half the battle. Getting your visa approved is half the struggle. Keeping up with the legal requirements of that visa afterwards is the other half.

I've seen students accepted by universities with full scholarships get rejected by US immigration anyway. Visitors generally have it a little easier, since they travel for a short period and return. Students have it harder — they need to complete an entire degree while maintaining their visa status the whole way through.

Through this service, I don't just help people complete the application. I give practical guidance on navigating US policy as it actually stands today. Whether you're a first-time visitor, a student, or an experienced traveller, policy changes are something most people don't account for — and I keep a close eye on policy updates and immigration news so the process stays smooth for the people I work with.

I didn't have someone to help me with the issues I faced as a student travelling to the United States. That's what I try to be for the people I work with now.

Not Just the US

I've also recently moved to South Africa myself, and I've been successfully navigating the visa and immigration processes here too — which has only deepened how I think about international immigration, from both sides of the process.

While my own lived experience is specifically with the United States, I'm not inexperienced when it comes to Canada and the United Kingdom. I have close family in both countries, and I've watched them go through the process of relocating there. I've spent real time learning from them about what that process actually looks like, and the best way to approach it — so there's genuine knowledge behind that guidance too, not guesswork.
